About Me

I am interested in how organizations make decisions under uncertainty, especially when disruptions, incomplete information, and complex system interactions make those decisions difficult. My work draws on operations research, simulation, and analytical modeling to study decision support in settings such as supply chains, maritime chokepoints, and human–AI collaboration.

My background spans mechanical engineering, management research, and data analytics. I have conducted research in both engineering and management contexts, including microfluidic systems for biomedical applications and digital-twin approaches for disruption management. These experiences motivate my broader interest in decision-making across technical and organizational systems.

Research Interests

Decision-making under disruption

How organizations can respond to geopolitical disruptions, logistical shocks, and uncertain operating environments, with a particular focus on supply chain resilience and maritime systems.

Digital twins and decision support

How simulation, surrogate models, and real-time information can improve operational decision support and help evaluate alternative responses.

Human–AI collaboration

How decision authority, oversight, and accountability should be structured when humans increasingly rely on AI tools in complex decision processes.

Additional engineering research experience

Microfluidic engineering, including continuous-flow, size-based fractionation of tumor spheroids and organoid-scale samples.
